MHPSS practice / Suicide research / Humanitarian strategy

Rethinking mental health. Strengthening communities. Transforming humanitarian practice.

Moses Bwesige Mukasa is a Ugandan mental health and psychosocial support practitioner and suicide researcher working at the intersection of community knowledge, research and humanitarian action. For more than a decade, his work has spanned conflict-affected, post-conflict and displacement settings across East, Central and Southern Africa.

Additional professional information

Current appointment and technical profile

Global Mission Transformation – Protection Specialist, Jesuit Refugee Service (JRS), Global Virtual Office, Global Mission Transformation Unit. His previous role was Inter-Regional MHPSS Officer for JRS’s Eastern and Southern Africa regions.

With over a decade of humanitarian experience, Moses works across community-based and community-led MHPSS, protection, suicide prevention and research, and strategic technical and programmatic support. He is a doctoral researcher jointly affiliated with Ghent University and Makerere University College of Humanities and Social Sciences.

His CV describes regional technical leadership across 14 countries, programme design and quality support, partnership coordination, applied research and evidence-to-practice guidance. Research shaped by lived context

Evidence matters most when it listens to the people living its questions.

Research, lived experience and community knowledge belong in the same conversation. That principle informs work on distress and coping in displacement settings, and the design of culturally grounded humanitarian responses.
