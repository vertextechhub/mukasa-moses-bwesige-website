Practice shaped by context

Moses Bwesige Mukasa is a Kampala-based Ugandan MHPSS practitioner, suicide researcher and humanitarian programme strategist. For more than a decade, he has worked across conflict-affected, post-conflict and displacement settings in East, Central and Southern Africa, as well as professional projects involving Europe. His work spans community-based mental health support, suicide prevention, humanitarian protection and programme strategy, with attention to culturally grounded, gender-responsive and community-led practice.

A JRS USA profile describes early volunteer experience at Uganda's national mental health hospital after undergraduate study, followed by humanitarian protection work in South Sudan and the Democratic Republic of the Congo. The available source does not give the volunteer dates or hospital name. The supplied career chronology then records roles in Ugandan public service and peacebuilding before psychosocial-support positions in the northern Uganda refugee response and later regional technical work. It includes employment, consultancies, volunteer and governance appointments; the dated record below preserves the source distinctions rather than inventing a single continuous job history.

Additional professional biography

MHPSS, protection and community-led practice

Moses Mukasa Bwesige is the Global Mission Transformation – Protection Specialist at Jesuit Refugee Service (JRS), having previously served as Inter-Regional MHPSS Officer for JRS’s Eastern and Southern Africa regions. With over a decade of experience in humanitarian settings, he has worked with and alongside United Nations agencies, governments, faith-based organisations, academic institutions, and international and national humanitarian and development organisations, providing strategic, technical and programmatic MHPSS advisory and on-site practice support.

His expertise spans community-based and community-led mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), suicide-related behaviour prevention and response research, and the bidirectional integration of MHPSS across humanitarian and development sectors. His extensive MHPSS and protection experience includes research, assessments, programme design and evaluation, technical guidance, and capacity strengthening through training, coaching and mentoring.

Moses’s research examines suicide-related risks and behaviours, coping mechanisms, and cultural understandings of mental health and psychosocial wellbeing among displaced populations, particularly South Sudanese refugees.

He is a doctoral researcher jointly affiliated with Ghent University in Belgium and Makerere University College of Humanities and Social Sciences (CHUSS), School of Psychology, Uganda. His academic background includes master’s qualifications in Public Health and Counselling Psychology.

Moses is the author of Reflexive Training: Reimagining Trainers of Trainers in Mental Health and Psychosocial Support. The book challenges foundational assumptions in Training of Trainers (ToT) practice in MHPSS. Drawing on critical pedagogy, anthropology and reflexive practice, it presents reflexive training as an ethical and political practice.

His work also spans suicide prevention, refugee mental health, psychosocial wellbeing and substance use treatment. His research, publications and contributions include authored and co-authored research, institutional resources, and other works with attribution stated in the publications and resources library.

Moses champions evidence-informed, culturally grounded and locally led approaches to MHPSS. He advocates for epistemic justice and systems that centre the knowledge, agency and leadership of affected communities.

CV supplement

Professional profile and leadership

Moses Bwesige Mukasa is a mental health and psychosocial support practitioner, suicide researcher and humanitarian programme strategist with more than ten years of professional experience across refugee and conflict-affected settings in East, Central and Southern Africa.

His experience includes regional technical leadership across 14 countries, programme design and quality support, staff capacity strengthening, partnership coordination, applied research and evidence-to-practice guidance. He has managed Uganda operations, coordinated multi-partner programmes, supported proposal and budget development, undertaken donor prospect research and served as Board Chair of a community-based sociotherapy organisation.

His professional work focuses on locally led, ethical and accountable psychosocial support for children, young people, families and communities. He is a doctoral candidate at Ghent University and Makerere University, with research interests in refugee mental health, suicide prevention, coping and the integration of mental health and psychosocial support into peacebuilding.

The CV lists the Global Mission-Oriented Transformation role as Protection Technical Specialist, June 2026–Present, and the Inter-Regional MHPSS Officer role as April 2023–Present. Both are shown as current in the CV. Their relationship is not explained; the roles and dates have not been reconciled with the earlier website chronology.
